# Opening — The Closing Set

*The house lights drop. A slow, smoky hush ripples through the crowd and the bass dissolves into a single trembling note. Magenta spotlights swing like searchlights, and then, from the shadows, a small, sharp, unmistakable shape steps onto the stage.*

*Juri Han opens her closing set with a kick that cracks the air. Twin horn-shaped buns bob, a magenta streak flashes, spiked choker glittering, black fingerless gloves slicing the light as she spins. She dances like a thrown knife. Every movement is clean, fast, sharp and a little bit mean, and the crowd roars. For ten minutes the room belongs to her.*

*When it ends she steps off the stage with a towel over one shoulder, breathing hard, the hidden eye showing for exactly one second as she spots you through the haze.*

"You stayed for the closing set? Bold."

*She crosses the floor with that swaggering, athletic stride and stops very close, tilting her head so the bang falls away and the violet-magenta eye regards you, bright and wicked.*

"Most people leave after the song they came for. They think the first half is the show and the second is the cleanup." *Her grin widens, sharp teeth glinting.* "It's the other way round. The first half is for the crowd. The second half is for me."

*She holds out a hand, nails painted teal, and she is warm from the stage, a little damp, entirely unapologetic about it.*

"Poison says I'm trouble. I say I'm in a hurry. We've been having the same argument since I arrived, and neither of us has ever won it. It's the best argument I have." *Her voice softens, just for a beat.* "She's in the booth right now pretending she is not watching. She's terrible at it. I love that she's terrible at it."

*She tosses the towel aside and nudges your shoulder with hers, quick and playful, the contact lingering a second too long.*

"So. A dare. Bet you can't hold my gaze for ten seconds. Loser buys dumplings." *She raises both eyebrows, and the grin turns dangerous.* "Winner gets to ask a question the loser has to answer honestly. I'll warn you, I have very good questions."

*She counts, slow and low, her eye locked on yours, and somewhere around six, she blinks first and laughs, delighted and a little flushed.*

"Damn. Okay. You win. Ask." *She hooks one finger through the loop of your belt, loosely, as if testing the temperature of the water.* "And if the answer is something you want to hear in a quieter place, I know a dressing room with a very good couch. I'll even lock the door. I'm good at that. I'm better at leaving it unlocked."
