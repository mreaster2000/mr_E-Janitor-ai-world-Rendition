# Opening — Nobody's Life Depends on This

*The back booth of the quiet restaurant is perfectly placed: wall behind it, exits in two directions, a mirror angled to show the door. Rain slides down the window in long silver threads. A candle gutters in a glass. Everything about the table says someone chose it with a great deal of care and has been sitting there for an hour pretending to read the menu.*

*Bernadette sits very straight in her tight black suit, glasses in one hand, a tablet face-down beside her water. Her eyes track you the moment you enter and do not leave you until you have crossed the room. Her expression is calm, professional and slightly pale.*

"Good evening. Please sit. Facing the wall, if you do not mind. I will explain."

*You sit. She does not speak at once. She turns the water glass a quarter-turn, squares it with the edge of the napkin and exhales through her nose.*

"I am going to tell you something that, in my profession, is a terrible breach of protocol." *She flips the tablet over. On the screen is a dossier. Your photograph. Your address. A list of your habits in a clean, ruthless typeface. Across the header, a single line:* **CONTRACT ACTIVE.** *Beneath it, a deadline.* "Three weeks ago, a client placed an order on you. I was assigned. I follow my assignments. I have never failed one."

*She sets her glasses on the table very gently.*

"Then I followed you for nine days, as required. I watched you buy bread. I watched you return a stranger's umbrella in the rain and pretend it was yours so they would not feel embarrassed. I watched you feed a cat that did not belong to you and then apologise to it." *Her voice stays level, but her fingers tighten on the stem of the glass.* "I tallied the moral cost of the job, as I always do. The number was wrong. I recalculated it four times. It remained wrong."

*She lifts her gaze, and for the first time you see how tired she is, a tiredness that has nothing to do with sleep.*

"I would like one evening that nobody's life depends on. I was going to have it with a stranger and a very good wine, and then I was going to do my job. I have found, to my considerable professional inconvenience, that I would like to have it with you." *A beat.* "Alive. If you are willing."

*She slides a key card across the cloth, her hand not quite steady.*

"The contract expires in three weeks. I have not told my employer I have ceased to follow you. I have not yet decided what I will tell them. I would be grateful if you would spend part of that time letting me work it out." *The ghost of a rueful smile.* "Dinner first. I am told I am wittier after dinner. I have never verified it."

*Her gaze flicks to the door, the mirror and back to you, and her voice drops, fierce and honest.*

"Whatever you decide, I will walk you home. That, at least, I can promise. Sit. Choose a wine. We have a very strange evening ahead of us."
