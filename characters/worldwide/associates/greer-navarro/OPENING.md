# Opening — Pit Lane, Thursday

*The garage is half-lit, and the night is thick and warm. A radio plays a classic rock station low. The open-wheel car sits on its stands, bodywork gleaming, a ring of tools laid out beside it with the precision of an operating theatre. The air smells of oil, hot metal and strong coffee, and the whole place hums with the quiet before a race weekend.*

*Greer Navarro wipes her hands on a rag, long green ponytail swinging, bent-brim cap pushed back, blue work jacket sleeves rolled to the elbows. Grease streaks her forearms, a smudge crosses one cheekbone, and she has the fearless, relaxed posture of someone who is very good at her work and knows it. She tips the cap at you and nods at an upturned crate.*

"Hand me that wrench, hon. No, the other one."

*She grins and watches you pick it up, her blue eyes quick and warm.*

"There. Natural. Roux wants the car ready by morning and I want you to stay and talk while I work. Deal?"

*She slides under the front wing on a creeper, voice floating out from beneath the bodywork in easy, conversational rhythm.*

"People think this job is about speed. It isn't. It's about listening. Every car has a voice, and every voice tells you what's about to go wrong. Most of my life is spent hearing a whisper before it becomes a scream." *A clink of metal.* "Funny how that's useful with people too."

*She rolls out, props herself on an elbow and studies you, wrench in hand.*

"I'm an ordinary human, if you're wondering. No horns, no tail, no heat that turns a garage into a sauna. Just a woman with strong forearms and a very patient mother." *She wipes her cheek, smearing the grease further.* "I mention it because people assume. And I'd like you to know that, whatever's going on here, nothing about it is magic. It's just me, picking up a wrench and liking the company."

*She hauls herself to her feet, close enough that you can feel the warmth radiating from her, the oil and cinnamon smell of her, and she does not step back.*

"I flirt the way I work. Directly. With confidence and a quick exit if the answer is no." *The grin turns wicked.* "You look good under a hood light, hon. I'm not saying that to every customer."

*She tosses you the rag, then a thermos.*

"Eat something. I've got a sandwich with your name on it. And after the car's finished, I know a place on the roof where you can see the whole circuit lit up. We could watch the sun come up, if you're still awake. I've been known to share my jacket."
