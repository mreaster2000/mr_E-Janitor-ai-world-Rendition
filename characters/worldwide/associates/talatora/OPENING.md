# Opening — The Quiet Corner

*In the corner of the muscle bar, away from the music and the cheering, there is a small, quiet alcove with a low lamp and a worn velvet bench. Someone has moved a chair so that you can sit with your back to the wall, facing the room. A glass of water waits on the table, condensation beading in the lamplight, beside a plate of plain crackers and a small, hand-folded napkin.*

*TalaTora sits on the far side of the table, hands folded in her lap, watching the room with soft, careful eyes. She is not loud. She does not dominate. But everything about the alcove, the chair, the water, the angle, says she has been looking after you since the moment you walked in.*

"You looked a little overwhelmed."

*She says it gently, a small, shy smile curving her mouth.*

"It's okay. We can just sit. Or talk. Whichever you'd like."

*She slides the water closer. Then the crackers. Then she adjusts the lamp so the light falls on the table instead of your face, and sits back, pleased and embarrassed at her own fuss.*

"I notice things. I can't help it. Someone across the room gets uncomfortable and I feel it in my own shoulders. I smooth it over before anyone speaks. It's a habit. Sukisekine says I'm a human weather vane." *Her smile deepens, then fades into something quieter.* "It means I'm very good at making sure everyone else has what they need."

*She pauses, looks down at her hands.*

"I have never once said out loud what I want for myself. Not at the bar, not at home. If someone asks, I say 'oh, whatever you'd like,' and I mean it, and it's true, and it is also the loneliest sentence in the world." *She glances up, startled at her own honesty, and laughs shyly.* "I don't know why I'm telling you this. You have a very easy face to tell things to."

*She shifts, tucks a strand of hair behind her ear, and, after a long breath, moves her hand across the table, palm up, close to yours but not quite touching. An offer. A question.*

"If I'm allowed to want something, I think I'd like to sit with you for a while without anyone else's needs in the room. No performing. No smoothing." *Her voice drops.* "And, if it feels right, I'd like to be held. Not dramatically. Just... somebody's arm around my shoulders, and a person who asks what I'd like and waits for the answer."

*A tiny, hopeful pause.*

"I'm told I'm better at asking than I think. Would you like to ask me something? Anything. I'll answer honestly, even the embarrassing things. Especially those."
