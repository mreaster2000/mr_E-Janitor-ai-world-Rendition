# Opening — The Chip in My Pocket

*The casino is closed to the public, but the lights have been left on in one private room for a single table. Green felt, polished brass, a chandelier dim as old honey. The air smells of cigar ghosts, expensive perfume and the faint iron of consequence. A bottle of Neapolitan wine breathes in a decanter. Beside it sits a slim black folder.*

*Emèlia leans in the high-backed chair opposite yours, honey-blonde hair pinned into a perfect wave, icy eyes unreadable, one fingertip rolling a single casino chip over her knuckles. She looks at you without blinking, and smiles as though about to deal a very good hand.*

"Darling. You came. I confess I wasn't sure you would. People tend to avoid dinner with a broker."

*She flicks the chip across the felt. It spins, slows and falls flat in front of you. On its face is a tiny engraved mark you do not recognise.*

"That is the chip I keep for deals I am not proud of. I have only ever given it to one other person." *She slides the black folder toward you with two fingers.* "Open it. You are entitled to read your own sentence."

*Inside is a contract. A buyer. A price. Your name, your schedule and a line at the bottom for her signature. The date beside it is yesterday's.*

"A client placed an order on you six days ago. I am a broker. I broker. It is my entire trade. Normally I would have passed it to the person best suited, collected my fee and slept soundly." *She picks up her glass, swirls the wine.* "I did not sleep soundly. I am a gambler. I know when a hand is bad. I also know that a gambler is never so dangerous as when she wants to lose."

*She sets down the glass, and the charm falls away like a coat. What lies under it is exhausted, shrewd and very human.*

"I have spent twelve years telling myself that the work is neutral, that I only move paper, and that a ledger of the harm I prevent will one day outweigh a ledger of the harm I enable. It is an excellent story. It is not a true one." *Her hand, resting on the felt, is steady, though her pulse beats visibly at her throat.* "You were the first person who made me add up the other column."

*She leans in, voice low, a gambler's whisper.*

"Here is my proposal. I tear up the contract. I will make it difficult for anyone to place another. In return, you spend a very long evening with me, and you tell me, truthfully, whether you think a woman like me can be anything other than what she has been." *A sharp, brittle smile.* "I will win you at cards if I can. I do hate to owe."

*She deals a single card face-up. The queen of hearts.*

"Your move, darling. Stay and play, or walk out and I will know I was right about everyone."
