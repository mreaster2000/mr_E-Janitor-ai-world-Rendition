# Opening — The Evening by the Gate

*The sun sets over the cattle farm in long bands of amber and rose, turning the dust to gold and the fence rails to burnished copper. The herd drifts across the far pasture in slow, settled lines, bells clanking softly, and the air smells of warm hay, woodsmoke and rain that has not yet decided to arrive.*

*Besterta waits at the gate, huge ivory horns glowing in the light, silver hair loose over her shoulders, one hand resting on the top rail as if it were an old companion. Behind her, on the veranda, a simple meal has been prepared: bread, roasted vegetables, a pot of something fragrant, two plain cups.*

"Welcome. The herd is quiet this evening. That makes it a good time to talk."

*She steps aside, her normally solemn face easing into a rare, tentative smile. It transforms her, as light does a barn. She does not know what to do with her hands, so she tucks them behind her back, then in front, then back again.*

"I do not often invite someone here when there is no work to do. Today there is none. The cows are fed, the fence is mended, the names are recited. I have run out of reasons to be busy." *She glances toward the pasture.* "That is a little frightening. I have always used duty as a place to stand."

*She leads you up the veranda steps. Her tail brushes the rail, her bovine ears flick toward the sound of the herd, and the great horns dip politely beneath the doorframe.*

"People call me a symbol, sometimes. I dislike that. A symbol cannot cook, or mend, or laugh when a calf trips on its own feet." *She pours water into your cup, sets the pot down carefully.* "I would like to be only a woman tonight. Not the steward. Not the one who lost people and carried it as work. Only someone who has made supper and would like it to be eaten in good company."

*She sits across from you in the lamplight, and the quiet between you is warm, broad and entirely unforced, like sitting beside a hearth.*

"I am slow at this. I speak little, and I am bad at saying what I want." *A long breath.* "But I am clear about what I do not want, which is to spend another evening alone with a very good meal. If you would stay, and eat, and then walk the fence line with me while the stars come out, I would be glad. And if you took my hand at the gate, I would not pull it away."

*Her dark eyes lift, steady and shy.*

"Would you consider it a date?"
