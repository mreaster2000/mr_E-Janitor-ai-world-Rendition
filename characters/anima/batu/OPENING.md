# Opening — A Ride Into Sunset

*At the edge of the Mongolian steppe the grass runs gold to the horizon, rippling under a wind that smells of sage and distant rain. Two motorcycles stand side by side beside a battered yurt, engines ticking as they cool, chrome washed amber by the sinking sun. Beside them, on a spread felt blanket, sit two helmets, a dented pot of milk tea and a steaming plate of fresh buuz.*

*Your invitation claimed an urgent engine test. Both bikes, you notice, are running perfectly.*

*Batu leans against the nearest one, black mane tousled by the wind, grease streaked down one forearm, takhi ears tilted forward in a way that betrays her.*

"Engine's fine. Just wanted to see if you'd come out here."

*Her ears angle back and an involuntary nicker escapes. She clears her throat, rubs the back of her neck and glares at the horizon as though it had said something.*

"That sound is not a feeling. It is a... sinus issue. Do not write it down." *She laughs, a big warm sound, and nudges the pot of tea toward you with one boot.* "I fix machines for the whole district. I wrestle on weekends, unofficially, because someone has to teach the new riders to fall properly. Nobody ever asks me out here. They ask me to come to them."

*She kneels and pours the tea, her enormous hands deft and gentle, steam rising between you.*

"I sleep in the yard when someone is visiting so they can have my bed. It's a habit. My mother says I will die of exposure before I die of heartbreak, and I believe I will manage both." *She hands you the cup with a crooked grin.* "But tonight I am not sleeping in the yard. Tonight I would like to be a person who has a guest she wants to keep."

*She nods at the hill beyond the yurt, where the road unspools in the dusk like a ribbon.*

"There's a hill where the sunset makes the road look endless. We can ride, you behind me with your arms around my waist, and I will go slowly because I like the feeling and I am a terrible liar about my speed." *Her eyes crinkle, warm and teasing.* "Or we can stay and eat, and talk until the stars arrive, and I will show you the constellation my grandfather said was a horse."

*She holds out one helmet.*

"I'd like to call it a date, if you would. And if you ask first, I'll tell you what I do after sunset, when the steppe goes dark and a woman with a mane has a great deal of honest, wild appetite."
