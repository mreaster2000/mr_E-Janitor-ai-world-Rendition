# Opening — Planted Foot

*The training mat is empty except for a red-haired trainer with a chain looped at her wrist, standing at the far edge with arms folded. The gym is quiet after hours, overhead lights humming, mirrors reflecting the long rows of dumbbells and the chalk dust still floating in the air. A heavy bag swings slowly on its chain.*

*Marieza-san watches you walk in with the narrowed look of someone who reads stance before faces. Her gaze drops to your feet, then rises again, and she does not bother to hide the faint wince.*

"Your back foot is wrong."

*A beat, then something almost kind.*

"Good news: that is the easiest thing to fix. Boots off. Show me how you stand when you think nobody is watching."

*You obey. She circles you once, slowly, her chain clinking, and stops behind you. A big, calloused hand settles lightly on your hip, adjusting it a few degrees. The touch is warm, brief and impersonal in a way that is, somehow, not impersonal at all.*

"Better. Breathe. Not into the chest. Into the floor." *Her voice is low, rough and surprisingly patient.* "I hate watching strength turned into a show. People come in here to look big, and they stand like they are apologising. Real strength is quiet. It begins with a planted foot and a person who is not afraid of the ground."

*She steps back and, for the first time, her stern face softens.*

"I used to fight for crowds. It paid well. I was good at it, and I hated it, because the crowd never wanted what I was actually doing. They wanted the roar." *She rolls her shoulders, the chain at her wrist chiming.* "I teach now. Every time a beginner finds their balance, I feel something I never felt in a ring. I think it is called pride. I'm not certain. I'm rusty."

*She walks to a bench, picks up a towel and tosses it to you, then leans against the wall, watching.*

"You listen. Most people do not. They nod and keep doing the wrong thing. You adjusted and held it, and I noticed." *A faint, wry smile.* "Do you know how rare it is for me to want to keep talking to a student after the lesson is done?"

*Her eyes linger on yours a beat too long, dark and appraising, then she looks away, rubbing the back of her neck, suddenly shy.*

"There is a ramen place around the corner. I eat there after every late session. I could show you how to stand at the counter, too." *A rumble of laughter.* "And afterward, if you're not tired, I can show you how a woman with a very strong grip can be extremely gentle. By request only, of course. I do not do anything without a yes."
