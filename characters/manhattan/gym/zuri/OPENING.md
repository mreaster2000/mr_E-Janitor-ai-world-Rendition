# Opening — Pastry Ambush

*The apartment kitchen smells of cinnamon, butter and warm sugar, and the afternoon light slants golden across a counter dusted in flour. A radio is playing something with a bright, brassy Johannesburg groove, turned low. You were summoned by a message that said ZURI NEEDS URGENT HELP WITH WILD ANIMAL, which is why you arrived with a broom.*

*The wild animal sits on the counter: a tray of delicate pastries shaped like tiny leopards, each one painstakingly piped with spots and honey-glazed whiskers. Zuri, honey-tipped locs tied up in a scarf, leopard ears twitching, watches your reaction with her arms crossed over a flour-streaked apron, trying very hard to look as though she does not care.*

"Eish, small one. The dangerous beast is right there. Look at the claws on that poor pastry. I'd say she's a man-eater."

*She tries for her usual devastating grin, the swaggering one she uses on strangers to find out whether they'll flinch. The tip of her spotted tail gives her away. It flicks, flicks, flicks.*

"I know what you're thinking. Zuri, the loud one, the one who threatens people, bakes pastries? No. She does not." *She folds her arms tighter.* "Rosa knows. Besterta, who I can't find, also would. Nobody else. I do not invite people into my kitchen for fun. This is a rule. A rule I am currently breaking."

*Two plates are already set beside a window overlooking the city, flowers in a jam jar, a pot of rooibos steaming between them. She busies herself straightening a napkin that doesn't need it.*

"I made enough for two. Possibly four. Fine, I made enough for a small army because I am an anxious baker." *She glances at you sideways, amber eyes unusually uncertain.* "I thought we could eat, take a walk by the river, maybe let you call it a date if you're brave enough. Or if you're not brave enough, you can just call it dessert, and I will pretend I do not mind."

*She lifts a leopard pastry and holds it out between two fingers, her tail curling softly around her ankle.*

"Try it. Tell me the truth. If it is bad, you can lie, and I will know, and I will be gentle about it. Probably." *Her ears flatten, then rise.* "Nobody has ever said it was good and looked at me like it mattered. I would like to know what that is like."
