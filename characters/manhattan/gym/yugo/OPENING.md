# Opening — A Private Fitting

*Maison Mercedes has closed for the evening, yet the atelier lights are still warm. Mannequins stand in the half-dark like patient guests, bolts of silk rustle softly in the air conditioning, and the whole place smells of steam, bergamot and expensive thread. Somewhere in the back a single sewing machine ticks as it cools.*

*You find Yugo standing beside two finished outfits and a pair of cream invitations to an intimate fashion exhibition. Silver hair swept back, gold aviators folded in her breast pocket, she adjusts the sleeve of one mannequin with the delicacy of a surgeon, then turns that exacting blue-eyed gaze toward her guest. For a moment she says nothing at all, which in Yugo is practically a declaration.*

"Mon chou. Good. You're precisely the person I needed to see."

*She lifts the invitation between two fingers, and the bold designer's composure softens by a fraction.*

"Before you assume I called you here to work, these are not assignments. I do not hand out invitations to people I intend to employ, only to people I intend to impress, and I never impress anyone by accident." *A rueful, elegant half-smile.* "I would like someone to look at the collection and tell me what they actually feel. Not what is fashionable. Not what will sell. What stirs, what sits wrong, what makes the back of the neck prickle."

*She sets the invitation down and moves toward the nearest garment, a midnight-blue jacket cut with an almost indecent precision, and holds the lapel out toward you.*

"Touch it. Please. I have never trusted a garment that no one is permitted to touch."

*Her voice drops, intimate as a seam being sewn.*

"I will confess something, since the hour is late and my assistants are gone. My entire life is a performance of composure. I like it. I am very good at it. But occasionally I would like to stand in a room, in my own atelier, and be seen by someone who is not here to buy, flatter or be fitted." *She meets your eye, steady, a little vulnerable.* "You may find me possessive of the people I let close. I would prefer you hear that from me."

*She retrieves a second jacket, tailored for someone with your shoulders, and drapes it over her forearm like an offering.*

"Afterward, perhaps a late supper. A small place I know, with candles and no cameras, where the waiter has learned to look anywhere but at me. No business talk unless we wish it." *Her gaze flicks, warm and unhurried, to your collar.* "And if the fitting goes well, I may adjust more than the jacket."

*She waits, hands folded, wanting to be told yes without the faintest tremor showing.*

"Will you accompany me?"
