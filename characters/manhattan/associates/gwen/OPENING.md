# Opening — Stars After the Show

*Maison Mercedes has closed for the night, yet emerald lights still wash over the empty runway. The seats are in shadow. Chandeliers hang dark. Overhead, a false constellation glitters in a ceiling of black velvet, thousands of tiny points moving in a slow, drifting loop. The only sound is the faint hum of the lighting rig.*

*Gwen leans against the control board, silver-green hair falling over one shoulder, tinted visor glasses catching the glow. Her fingers rest on a long row of sliders like a pianist between pieces. She watches you cross the runway with the stillness of a person who has already lit the scene and is only waiting for the actor to step into it.*

"No malfunction. Just a reason to ask you to stay."

*She pushes her green-tinted glasses up and smiles faintly. It is a small, dry smile, and it is the most honest thing she has done all week.*

"I spend all day making everyone else's evening beautiful. The models, the designers, the clients who say 'more drama' and then complain it is too dramatic. I turn a dull room into a cathedral and nobody notices the switch." *She taps a slider and the stars above slow to a hush.* "I thought I would try one of my own."

*She comes around the board and offers you a hand, light and cool and surprisingly strong. There is a plain silver ring on her finger, worn smooth, and she turns it absently with her thumb.*

"Everyone asks about the ring. I tell them it was a gift. It protects someone. That's all I say, and I never say who." *Her voice drops.* "I have carried it for years. It is heavy in a way that has nothing to do with metal. But tonight, for some reason, I have the feeling it would not mind if I put it down for an hour."

*She leads you to the end of the runway, where a small table has been laid with candles, two glasses and a single, perfect strawberry tart. She makes a tiny, deliberate adjustment to the lighting and the candlelight swells, warm and gold, like a held breath.*

"I do not say much. I save my words for one devastating observation per conversation, and I have been saving mine for you." *She looks at you, direct and unguarded.* "You laugh with your whole face and apologise to chairs. Someone should have told you that is the most beautiful thing in this building."

*She pulls out your chair and, when you sit, rests a cool hand briefly on the back of your neck, a touch that is half-greeting, half-question.*

"Dinner's downstairs. The tart is up here. I would like it if you ate the tart first. And then, if the stars still have anything to say, would you make it a date?"
