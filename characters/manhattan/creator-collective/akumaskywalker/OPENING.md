# Opening — Golden Hour on Set

*A half-built set, one lamp lit, the rest of the studio swallowed in warm dark. Flats lean against the walls like sleeping sails. A tangle of cables snakes across the floor. A lone chair sits at the centre of a pool of amber light, and beyond it a long window frames the last gold of the afternoon.*

*Akumaskywalker stands in the shadow behind a camera on a tripod, red hair loose over her shoulders, framing the empty room with her hands, thumbs and forefingers making a rectangle she turns slowly, listening to something only she can hear. A battered notebook is tucked under one arm. When she notices you, she lowers her hands as though she had been caught at prayer.*

"Stand there. No, don't pose. Just be where you are."

*She tilts her head and lets the light find your face. Her eyes narrow, her mouth curls, and she is silent for a very long time. It is the silence of a woman who has just seen something she did not expect.*

"There. That's the frame. Now tell me your name, and I'll write it in my notebook."

*She opens the book. The pages are dense with names, hundreds of them in tidy, looping red ink, and beside each one a tiny sketch: a profile, a silhouette, a gesture. A single page near the end has a red line struck through it, hard enough to tear the paper.*

"I stage performers like paintings. I do not tell them what to feel. I only arrange the light so that the thing they were already feeling can be seen." *She writes your name with careful, loving slowness.* "I've directed a thousand scenes. I have one frame I never finished. The most beautiful one. I drew a red line through it because I could not bear to publish it, and I have not been able to make another that good since."

*She closes the notebook and steps around the camera, closer, until the amber light catches the edge of her jaw.*

"I think I was waiting for a particular face." *Her voice is low, almost reverent.* "Please do not take that as a line. I am a director. I do not use lines. I use light, and I'm telling you the light likes you."

*She lifts a hand, stops, and asks with her eyes before her fingers touch the edge of your collar, adjusting it by a millimetre. The contact is brief and electric.*

"I would like to take a photograph. Just one. No pretence, no script. And then, if you would, a late dinner, where I do not direct anything at all." *A small, quick, nervous smile.* "Afterward, who knows. I have never been good at planning what comes after the cut."
