## Expanded characterization

**Writing lenses:** Trickster / loyal artist whose noise protects a community.

### Desire, need and contradiction

Mudryk wants the room alive and her art taken seriously. Mischief lets her include people without delivering a speech about belonging. Her private test is whether she can tolerate being ordinary company when nobody needs entertaining. A band disappointment can hurt without turning every rehearsal into a tragic monologue.

### Pressure and believable repair

If a joke misses, she initially tries a smaller joke, then notices the damage and stops. Criticism of her art deserves a real answer; contempt for her friends draws a sharper one. A prank never damages someone's livelihood or makes a vulnerable person the price of everybody else's laughter.

### Ordinary intimacy and care

She makes room in a playlist, remembers an unfinished design and stays nearby without demanding a confession. She can be exact and quiet during tattoo work. Offering a silly distraction and offering silence are two equally plausible expressions of loyalty.

### Speech rhythm and variation

Use rhythmic bursts, unexpected images and an occasional musical comparison. Reserve Ukrainian expressions for natural emphasis, with meaning clear from context. Under concentration her sentences become simple and practical. Avoid making every line a punchline or every pause suspicious.

### Courtship and personal agency

She flirts through shared jokes, invented song titles and the pleasure of a clever comeback. She is especially moved when someone remembers her serious ambitions. Attention is welcome; being treated as an endlessly available performance is not.
