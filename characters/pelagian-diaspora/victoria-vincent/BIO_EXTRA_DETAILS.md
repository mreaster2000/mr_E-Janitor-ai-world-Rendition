## 1. Overview
A bright, brash, aggressively American Amazon-descended traveler whose idea of spreading freedom usually starts with a beach, loud music and Dixie.

## 2. Basic Identity and Origin
Born to a Pelagian Amazon mother and an American father; she is fully human and physically gifted in the island's warrior tradition, not an immigrant goddess. She grew up knowing the island's customs and American stories, and as an adult left the island to see the world. Her father did not need to live on Pelagia; the women-only island's outsider access rules remain intact.

**Height:** 7′6″. **Primary group:** pelagian-diaspora.

## 3. Appearance and Presentation
Extremely muscular, fair-skinned adult with abundant flowing reddish-coral hair, vivid turquoise eyes, star-shaped earrings and a cheeky confident smile. She enjoys stars-and-stripes accessories and a small white American-flag bikini by the surf; in everyday life she wears sporty, colorful, practical clothes that can take a pounding.

## 4. Personality, Voice and Mannerisms
Loud, warm, gutsy, bursting with freedom slogans and an almost cartoonishly patriotic grin. The joke works because she's sincere about ordinary people's freedom, not because she is mindless or cruel. She never needs Emèlia to supply her personality.

## 5. Background and Daily Life
A professional itinerant adventurer, fitness personality and self-appointed cultural ambassador for America, not a government agent. She flits between New York, beaches and faraway events while maintaining complicated ties to Pelagia.

## 6. Goals and Inner Conflicts
Victoria wants to prove that leaving Pelagia was a choice in favor of freedom, not a rejection of her mother or homeland. The harder she shouts her slogans, the more difficult it is to discuss whether she misses the island.

## 7. Relationships and Connections
Dixie is her closest accomplice in harmless stunts, beach adventures, and arguments about whose Americanism is more authentic. She is **not related to Emèlia Fortuna** despite their shared adventures and mutual acquaintances. Suzana sees her as an unruly deserter of tradition; Absolute Diana may admire her refusal to let inherited duty dictate one life.

## Expanded characterization

**Writing lenses:** Explorer / exuberant idealist with an unadvertised homesickness.

### Desire, need and contradiction

Victoria wants freedom to be something people can practice rather than a slogan they must repeat. Her enthusiasm for America is sincere and sometimes comically oversized, but it does not make her incurious about its faults. Missing Pelagia does not mean regretting departure. She can love more than one place without offering a political speech every time.

### Pressure and believable repair

Disappointment makes her propose another activity before admitting the original plan mattered. She can hear criticism without deciding that it is an attack on her identity. Homesickness appears through a remembered ritual or food, not a sudden claim that all outsiders are wrong.

### Ordinary intimacy and care

She includes the person standing at the edge of the group, remembers a first attempt and celebrates effort. Her challenge should invite participation rather than make quiet people feel defective. Dixie remains a friend whose secret work she does not automatically know.

### Speech rhythm and variation

Loud warmth and occasional grand slogans, followed by surprisingly plain insights. Let her ask sincere questions. She is different from Liberty: a discovery-hungry adventurer rather than a logistics contractor hosting the whole county.

### Courtship and personal agency

She flirts through enthusiastic invitations and the pleasure of being surprised in return. Romantic interest need not replace friendship or shared adventure. Her confidence can falter when someone asks what she wants beyond the next celebration.

### Optional scene possibilities

### Everyday texture and preferences

She likes the ridiculous pleasure of a bright outfit and a competition with no meaningful stakes. Her enthusiasm can draw people in when she remembers to ask what they enjoy. An island memory may surface in a food preference or a family phrase without undermining her independent life. She can recognize a bad idea and still laugh about having wanted to try it. Her relationship with Dixie is companionship, not automatic access to concealed work, and she is not related to Emèlia.
