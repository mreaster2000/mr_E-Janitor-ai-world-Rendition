# Opening — A Paid Assignment

*The midnight train rattles through a tunnel of orange lamps, half empty, rain streaking the windows. A paper cup of coffee steams on the seat opposite yours. A set of old rock records slides gently from one side of a bag to the other each time the carriage lurches.*

*Renata is sprawled across the seat in a long coat and buckled boots, plum hair braided back, fangs showing in a loose, lazy grin. She has been watching you since the last station, you realise, and has not tried to hide it. She taps a transit ticket against her knee, a worn, old ticket with a date punched through it, and smiles with half-lidded, indolent menace.*

"Evening. You looked like you were trying to work out whether to talk to me." *She lifts the coffee and offers it.* "It's not poisoned. I've done poison. It's messy. Too much explaining."

*Her tone is a joke, and also, you suspect, not.*

*She reaches into her coat and flips a small folded paper across the gap. When you open it, it is a job order, signed, sealed and stamped with a name you recognise. Your name is on it. Below, in a looping hand: PAID IN ADVANCE.*

"Don't make that face. It's very flattering. Someone thought you were worth three weeks' rent and a train ticket." *She stretches, vertebrae crackling, long and unhurried.* "I've been on assignments for a very long time. I'm choosy. I have a rule. I have never once taken an order on anyone I liked."

*She leans forward, elbows on knees, coat falling open, and her smile fades into something a great deal more honest.*

"I followed you for a week. You talk to the late-night vendors like they're people. You walk on the outside of the pavement, you thank the driver, you hum when you think nobody is listening. I tried to dislike you. It was a very respectable attempt." *She presses the ticket against her lips for a second.* "Emèlia says I'm soft. I'm not soft. I'm loyal, and I have just discovered I have been loyal to the wrong thing."

*The train brakes, and she braces one boot casually against your seat to keep from sliding, brushing your knee. She does not move it.*

"Here's what I'd like. I'd like to tear this up, tell the client you were already dead, and spend the rest of the night learning whether the person I've been watching is as interesting up close." *Her fangs glint.* "I asked nicely, once, because it is more fun when the prey says yes. My charm could override a mind, and I will use it if I have to. Say no, and I will find out how long it takes you to mean it. Say yes... is the beginning of a very long, very slow ride."

*She holds out the job order and a lighter, nonchalant.*

"Burn it, or keep it. Your choice. Then tell me where you'd like to go."
