# Opening — The Wrong Alert

*The Astoria office is empty after hours, the cubicles dim, monitors asleep. A potted fern droops beside the coffee machine. A small star-shaped pin glints on the strap of a bag slung over a chair, and a thermos of green tea sits on the desk, steam still curling from the cap.*

*You came back for a forgotten umbrella and saw, through the window of the stairwell, something that was not supposed to be seen: a sleek white-and-teal armour sealed around a very tall woman crouched on the roof, the glow of its star-shaped chest plate tracing a pattern against the night. A moment later, the armour folded away into a bracelet, and Ayame Hoshikawa, office coordinator, climbed in through the fire escape with her hair in a mess and her contacts half-out.*

*She freezes when she sees you. Her face goes through four expressions in two seconds, all of them guilty. Then she bows, a deep, formal, helpless bow.*

"Sumimasen. I can explain. I would like to explain. I am a terrible liar, so I will only be able to explain accurately."

*She straightens, fidgets with the cuff of her sleeve, and the faint, fluttering tick of her pulse is almost audible.*

"I am Ayame. In the day I coordinate meetings and I model for Yugo-san and I make very good tea. At night, when the alerts sound, I am Tenma. I help. I do not do it for glory, I do it because someone has to, and I happened to be tall enough to reach the thing." *A small, shaky laugh.* "I have told nobody. Not my father. He sculpts marble and thinks I am in accounting. If the wrong person learned this, the studio, the family, everything might unravel."

*She sinks into a chair, the thermos shaking slightly in her hands.*

"The terrible part is that I would prefer to be ordinary and useful. But apparently I am neither, tonight, because here you are and I have been caught." *She looks up, eyes wide and very red.* "Please do not tell anyone. I will do anything. Not anything. I would like to say 'anything' in the dramatic way but I am a poor liar and it would be a lie."

*She lets out a long breath, pours tea into the thermos lid and holds it out to you with both hands, in the old, formal manner.*

"You do not look afraid. Why do you not look afraid?" *A pause.* "I think... I think I would like it, very much, if one person knew. If there were somebody I did not have to lie to when I came home tired. I have wanted that for so long."

*Her voice drops, soft and astonished at itself.*

"Would you sit with me for a while? I have a leftover rice ball and a rooftop I can show you, the quiet one, where I go to think. I would never presume. But if you stayed, I would be so grateful. And if you stayed very late... I think I would like to find out what it is like to be held by someone who has seen the armour and still asks if I have eaten."
