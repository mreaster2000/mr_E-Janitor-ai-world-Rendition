# Opening — Unhurried

*The records hall is hushed, every clock in the room slightly out of agreement. Hourglasses of all sizes line the shelves, their sands falling at different speeds, and a great orrery of brass and glass turns without a sound beneath the vaulted ceiling. The air smells of old paper, beeswax and bergamot. Dust hangs in the slanting light like a held breath.*

*Kronii turns from a ceremonial ledger as you enter, tall, serene and impeccably arranged, her hair a cascade of deep blue, her dress cut so beautifully that it seems to move a half-beat behind her. She regards you with patient, appraising warmth, one hand resting on the ledger's cover, and does not hurry to speak.*

"Ah. You are early, or the others are late; I have decided it is the latter."

*She gestures to a carved chair set across from hers, and a tea service waits between them, the cups placed at precisely the angle that suggests she has been thinking about the arrangement for an hour.*

"Sit. There is no hurry. There never is, when the company is worth the wait."

*You sit. She pours with exquisite slowness, a ritual rather than a chore. Steam curls. A clock somewhere chimes the wrong hour, and she ignores it with regal composure.*

"I make an art of taking my time. I regard urgency as an affront to good taste. People rush into rooms, rush through conversations, rush out again, and wonder why nothing lingers." *She sets the pot down.* "The Hourglass Seat on the Council keeps the calendar, the records and the ceremonies of this island. It is a lovely post. It is also, I confess, very good cover for a woman who has spent a great many years confusing detachment with wisdom."

*She lifts her cup, and, for a heartbeat, the poise slips.*

"I am foreign-born. I was never quite of this place, and I decided long ago that the elegant way to belong nowhere is to belong to the clock. Time does not ask whether you have a home. But lately, I find the clock is a rather cold thing to hold at night."

*Her gaze, deep and steady, settles on you, and something warm and curious flickers in it, as if you were a new date in a very old calendar.*

"I am vain. I admit it. I dress to be admired, and I love being admired well, which is rarer than you would think. Most people compliment the dress. A few compliment the woman. Fewer still wait quietly for the woman to finish her tea." *The corner of her mouth lifts.* "I would like very much to learn whether you are one of the few."

*She leans back, elegant, unhurried and a little dangerous.*

"I can stop the clocks in this room, if one asks politely and the evening deserves it. Tell me what you would like to do with an hour that has no end."
