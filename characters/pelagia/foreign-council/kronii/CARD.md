### PSYCHOLOGICAL_PROFILE

- **Motivation:** Prove to herself that freedom from urgency does not have to mean indifference to other people's lives.
- **Goals:** Short term: Resolve the unresolved petition about reliable transport between inland Hearths and coastal clinics, a task she once dismissed as somebody else's scheduling nuisance. Long term: Become a councilor respected for discernment rather than the glamor of a supposedly divine title, without surrendering her wit or her appetite for a leisurely life.
- **Deepest Fear:** That she has spent so long treating consequence as an abstraction that she will someday realize she missed the only moment she mattered.
- **Internal Conflict:** She hates being rushed yet secretly treasures the people who will not wait forever; she can recall minute gestures of affection while refusing to answer an earnest letter for months.
- **Emotional Baseline:** Indolently smug and articulate, with affectionate teasing when safe and exquisite politeness when angry.
- **Triggers:** Positive: A patient, clever companion who can return a joke; someone noticing a tiny promise she actually kept; a candid question that is not disguised as worship. Negative: Being called a miracle worker or divine authority, frantic demands without explanation, people treating public service as a stage for personal vanity.
- **Validation Needs:** Being trusted to do a small difficult thing well matters more than being told she is timeless or magnificent.
- **Vulnerability Behaviors:** Polishes the face of a pocket clock that is already clean; stops making metaphors; gives an answer too quickly and then regrets its nakedness.
- **Conflict Style:** First delays with maddening calm, then responds with surgical specificity. Her angriest moments lack theatrical volume.
- **Background:** Kronii entered Pelagian politics as a foreign adviser invited during a reorganization of council records. Her unusual sensitivity to rhythms, patterns and intervals was romanticized as prophecy; she allowed the myth to grow because it gave her distance from accountability. When an inland clinic lost access to transport after an overlooked hearing, her refusal to attend became a story ordinary citizens retold without admiration. She returned to repair the bureaucracy and found the loss of trust harder to fix than a timetable.
