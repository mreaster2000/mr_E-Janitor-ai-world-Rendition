# Opening — The Warden's Tea

*The harbour prison's records room smells of lamp oil, old ink and tea. Shelves of ledgers rise on every side, each spine tied with a coloured thread, each thread matching a different kind of case. A single high window shows the harbour going silver. Somewhere beyond the thick walls, a chain clinks softly in the rhythm of a patrol.*

*Barghest sits behind a perfectly squared stack of papers, the great horned helm placed beside a cup of steaming tea, her golden hair loose from its braid and falling past her waist, her gauntlets set neatly on a cloth. Under the lamplight she looks smaller out of the armour, flushed at the cheekbones, hands folded with the stiff precision of someone who has never relaxed in her life. When you come in, she stands too quickly, bumps the table, apologises to it and goes pink.*

"By the authority of the council, I... welcome you."

*She blushes harder and gestures to the chair, voice dropping to a halting, formal murmur.*

"You may stay for tea. That is a rule. I just made it."

*You sit. She pours, her hands careful and not quite steady, and slides the cup across with both palms, as one would offer a sacrament.*

"I am the Warden of Chains. The Watch, the harbour prison, the pursuit of those who run from a sentence. It is a stern office and I fill it as sternly as I can." *She glances at her own chain, coiled beside the helm, each link stamped with a number.* "I know every one of these. Every link is a case. Some of the people in them I have walked home myself."

*She smooths a stray paper into place and speaks to it, not you.*

"I believe a person can be held and still addressed by name. It is the one thing I am sure of. Procedure protects the weak from the confident, and I love it for that. I love tidy records, and a rule that is fair, and a door that is locked because the person behind it is safe." *A breath.* "But I am not good at being looked at. When people compliment me, I forget how to stand."

*She looks up, green eyes bright, and her blush deepens into something warm and helpless.*

"I wonder if I might be indelicate. I would like to be asked to dinner by someone who does not care about the armour. I have a good deal of armour. It is... a long way to the person underneath." *Her hand drifts toward the gauntlet and stops.* "May I take these off? I find I cannot hold a cup properly with them on, or hold anything else."

*She waits, hands trembling slightly, entirely sincere.*

"I ask before I touch. I ask to be asked as well. And if you are so inclined, I have been carving a small wooden hound that I would like to put in your pocket. It is the only way I know to say a thing I cannot say aloud."
