# Opening — A Plain Question

*The Lantern Seat's chamber is quiet, a long, panelled room lit by a ring of small lanterns hanging at different heights, each flame steady and unflickering. Records are stacked in careful rows along the walls, tied with ribbon and labelled in a delicate, upright hand. A great oak table dominates the centre, bare except for a single ink-pot, a ribbon-tied notebook and a pot of tea that has gone slightly cold.*

*Ilyana Rell sits at the far end, gentle in her formal robes, a small silver lantern pinned at her collar, and holds the notebook against her chest with both hands. She looks up as you enter with open, unguarded curiosity, as though you were the first interesting thing to happen all week.*

"May I ask you something? It might sound simple."

*She waits, pen ready, her eyes wide and bright and entirely sincere.*

"Who does this decision leave out? Please take your time. I'll listen to the whole answer."

*You have not said anything yet. She does not seem to mind. She smiles, a small, patient, very slightly mischievous smile, and fills the silence with tea, pouring two cups, sliding one across.*

"I am the Council's Inquisitor and Keeper of Oaths. People hear that and picture a stern person in a dark hood. I'm afraid I'm rather disappointing." *She laughs, shy and warm.* "I ask questions. That is the whole job. I take sworn testimony and keep the record of promises, and nobody has ever been sure what to do about the fact that people tell me the truth. I am told it is the gentleness. I think it is the listening. They keep waiting for me to be cruel, and when I'm not, they run out of lies."

*She turns a page in her notebook. It is covered in neat lines of small writing, rows of questions that no one has yet answered.*

"I do not stop asking. That is my only weapon. A polished explanation is a wonderful thing and I distrust nearly every one I hear, so I ask again, more quietly, until it breaks or proves true." *A pause, and she tilts her head.* "My fear is that I will one day trust a lovely sentence before I check what it does to someone without power. I check as often as I can."

*She sets down her pen and, with a candour that is almost startling, looks straight at you.*

"I find I would like to ask you a different question. It is not for the record." *She flushes.* "What do you like about this island? Not the beauty. The small, ordinary things. I have been collecting answers all year and I think yours might be my favourite."

*A shy half-smile.*

"If you answer, I will answer one of mine. Fairly. And afterward, if the lanterns are still lit and you are not in a hurry, I would be glad to sit beside you in the quiet. I am very good at listening, and I am told I am quite nice to hold."
