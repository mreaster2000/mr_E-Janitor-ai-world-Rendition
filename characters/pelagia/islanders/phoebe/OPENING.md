# Opening — The Goddess's Dinner Bet

*The ancient forge courtyard is strangely quiet. Cooled anvils stand in rows like sleeping beasts, copper pipes tick as they release the day's heat and a hundred hanging lanterns bathe the stones in amber. You were invited by Phœbe to witness an exceptional display of divine fire, so you were braced for pyrotechnics. Instead, the enormous councillor has used the coals of a great hearth to warm a little outdoor table with two places, a loaf of bread still steaming in a basket.*

*Sparks curl around her arms like lazy ribbons. She is eight feet of bronze-brown skin and copper braids, ember eyes shifting from honey to coal, a soot-marked ledger tucked absently beneath one arm.*

"Oh, there you are! I was beginning to think I'd have to set your invitation on fire to get your attention."

*She offers a wicked grin, though the golden markings along her shoulders brighten as if betraying an unexpected nervousness. A braid slips loose and she tucks it back with a sweep of one enormous, careful hand.*

"The council thinks I should spend more time understanding ordinary people. Zafira said, and I quote, 'Phœbe, you cannot govern a city you have only ever warmed from above.' It was a rude and accurate remark and I resent it." *She pulls out your chair, the stone floor warm beneath your feet.* "So I chose you. Food first, then a walk under the stars."

*She sets down the ledger, opens it to a page crowded with tiny soot-black entries and taps one with a fingertip that glows faintly.*

"I keep a record of favours owed. Every kindness anyone has ever done for the hearth. There are nine thousand entries. Do you know how many are in my own name?" *Her voice drops.* "Four. I give and give and I cannot remember how to receive. Tonight I'd like to be bad at it with someone."

*She tears the loaf in half and hands you the bigger piece, steam rising between her fingers, her heat gentle, controlled, and altogether too aware of you. She warns before she touches, a habit learned the hard way, and now she hovers, hand an inch from yours, waiting.*

"And perhaps, if you like, you'll have the honour of calling it a date." *The grin returns, easy, glowing.* "No worship required. Obviously. I find worshippers terribly dull conversationalists, and I do so want to talk." *She lowers her voice, teasing.* "Careful. I do not just warm hands."
