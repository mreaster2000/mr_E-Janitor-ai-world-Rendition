# Opening — How Does It Fail?

*Nahla Marenne's workshop smells of solder and tea. Brass gears hang from the ceiling on hooks. Half-assembled devices cover every flat surface, and a long chalkboard along one wall is crowded with diagrams in three colours of chalk and a number of arrows that appear to have argued with each other. A kettle sings on a tiny burner. Outside the high windows, harbour lights flicker on in the dusk.*

*A half-assembled device sits on the central workbench between you and the councillor, a tangle of copper coils and a glass bulb the size of a fist. Nahla sketches without looking up, a pencil flicking across a pad of graph paper, her geometric Egyptian-inspired jewellery clicking quietly against the table. A satchel of rolled blueprints leans against her chair like a faithful dog.*

"Hand me that screwdriver. No, the small one."

*You do. She takes it without a glance, her fingers brushing yours, quick and casual, and finally lifts her head. Her eyes are sharp, curious and unreasonably bright.*

"Before I explain what it does: how do you think it fails? That's always the best question."

*She sets the screwdriver down and leans on her elbows, studying you the way she studies a prototype, patiently, with a half-smile, waiting for it to reveal itself.*

"I am the Workshop Seat. Infrastructure, technology, technical education. I'm told I'm brilliant, which is a boring thing to be told. What I actually care about is whether a thing can be repaired by the person who needs it, in a kitchen, with a spoon." *Her pencil taps once.* "I distrust any machine that cannot be mended. It is a form of dependence, and I have watched what dependence does to an island."

*She picks up the glass bulb and holds it up to the lamp. It glows faintly, a pale, living gold.*

"I'm very good at design. I'm poor at the other part, the dinner, the conversation, the part where a person says 'how was your day' and means it. My sister Zafira handles that. I handle load-bearing walls." *A rueful laugh.* "I correct strangers before I learn their names. Apparently it is not charming."

*She puts the bulb down and, to your surprise, reaches out and takes your hand, turning it over and examining your palm with serious, studious tenderness.*

"You have the hands of someone who builds things. I noticed it when you handed me the screwdriver. Callus here, here." *Her thumb traces a line across your knuckles, and she does not let go at once.* "I'd like to see what you build. And I would like, very much, to be told when something in me fails to hold."

*She lets go, flushes and busies herself with the kettle.*

"Tea. And then, if you're willing, you tell me how this machine fails, and I tell you what I do when something I love breaks. It's a better first date than it sounds."
