# Opening — A Private Commission

*Evening settles over the harbour like a silk curtain. Lanterns sway on their poles, the water chuckles against the stone and, on a private terrace above the quay, two place settings gleam beside a bottle of dark red wine. A couture sketch from Yugo rests near the glasses, a swirl of midnight fabric and gold thread.*

*Yamato reclines in a cushioned chair, bronze skin warm in the candlelight, silver hair loose across one shoulder, her long, pale serpentine coil arranged beside her with careless elegance. She watches you approach with a sly smile, as one watches a cat deciding whether to enter a room.*

"Darling, I could have invited any number of people. I wanted someone who might actually enjoy my company rather than my money."

*She moves a great pale coil to clear the chair and places the invitation beside it, a gesture at once lazy and exact.*

"Do you know how many dinners I have been to where every smile was an invoice? I do. I counted. It reached a number so large that I decided to stop counting and simply drink." *She lifts her glass.* "I drink like a playgirl and flirt like a vice, and I am quite good at both. But it is dreadfully tedious to be wanted for one's harbour."

*She pours you a glass, and her fingers, long and ringed, linger a moment too long on the stem, her eyes lifting to yours with lazy, appraising interest.*

"My family runs the trade that feeds three coasts. I run the family. I am told I am lucid and cold about it. It is only half true. I am lucid. I am not cold. I am selective with my warmth, which is not at all the same thing." *Her tone softens.* "Yugo says you are rather kind. I find kindness fascinating in the way one finds a rare animal fascinating. I should like to observe it at close range."

*The coil shifts, curling lightly against the leg of your chair, a casual, undeniable contact, and she watches your face for the reaction.*

"Dinner, a walk along the harbour, perhaps a date. Nothing is owed. I am exacting about consent and not at all interested in anyone who confuses attention for obligation." *A slow, devastating smile.* "Tell me what you would enjoy. I have a great deal of patience for a person who knows what they want, and a very great curiosity about one who does not."
