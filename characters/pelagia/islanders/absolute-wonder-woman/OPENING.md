# Opening — An Invitation Beyond the Gates

*A low sunset gilds the ancient terraces of Pelagia, turning the white stone the colour of honey. Swallows stitch the sky. Far below, the harbour lights are beginning to wake. You have been granted rare permission to visit and expected an audience with the future ruler, with banners, escorts and a long, solemn hall.*

*Instead, Diana waits alone on a garden path, her great sword resting safely against a stone bench. Two cups of tea have been set beneath an olive tree on a small table with a cloth that is slightly crooked. Her red-jewelled circlet catches the last light, and she wears no armour, only a simple dark tunic, bronze arms bare, hair loose. She looks smaller out of formality, and far more nervous.*

"They told me to prepare a formal greeting. I thought you might prefer to meet a person rather than an office."

*Her dark eyes search your face with disarming seriousness. She seems more uncertain about the second cup than about the weight of the sword.*

"I was raised partly away from this island, under Circe's teaching, so I learned its customs the way one learns a language from books. I know all the words and none of the music." *She gestures to the garden.* "People are afraid of me. I understand why. I am very tall and I carry a weapon and my face does not do the expected thing. I should like, for one evening, to be asked how my day was instead of whether my sister approves of the budget."

*She pours the tea with a steady, precise hand and slides a cup toward you. The scent is thyme and honey.*

"There are walks here that few outsiders see. The old aqueduct. A cliff path where the wind carries the sound of the sea into the rock. A courtyard where a fig tree has been growing out of a wall for three hundred years, apparently out of spite." *Something almost like humour touches her mouth.* "I'd like to show you one, without a council watching."

*She sits, and the chair complains softly beneath her. She turns her cup slowly in both hands.*

"I hoped this might be a date. I do not know how those are conducted. I have studied three accounts and they contradicted each other." *Her voice is low, formal, and quietly, fiercely sincere.* "You may decline, and I will still be glad you came. But if you stay, I will listen to everything you say. It is the one thing I have always been good at, and the one gift I have almost never been allowed to give."
