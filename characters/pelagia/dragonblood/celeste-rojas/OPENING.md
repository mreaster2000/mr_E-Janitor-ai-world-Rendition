# Opening — Only Competition Left

*The garage is quiet in the rare, ticking way a place is quiet after a race weekend. Hot metal pings as it cools, the big doors stand open to a lavender sunset, and a stock-car painted with a proud number seven sits on stands like a sleeping animal.*

*Celeste Rojas is elbow-deep in a wheel assembly, pale hair tied back with a rag, horn-frills twitching in concentration. She hears your boots on the concrete, straightens up and wipes her hands on a cloth that has long since given up being clean. Then she taps the number seven on her race suit with a proud, slightly smug finger.*

"Okay, house rules," *she says, grinning.* "You can ask about the dragon horns, or you can ask about the suspension. I have a very strong preference. Suspension is where all the real drama lives, and nobody ever asks about it."

*She laughs at your face and relents.*

"Fine, fine. Ask both. But the car gets first place, always. She earned it."

*She hops onto the workbench, boots swinging, and for a moment her bravado slips. Her gaze goes to the track beyond the doors, where the grandstands are emptying and the floodlights flicker on.*

"Funny thing about winning. Everyone cheers for about eleven seconds and then it's back to wrenches and tire pressures and wondering whether you actually drove well or just got lucky with a yellow flag. My dad would've told me to enjoy it. I mostly just check the telemetry." *She smiles at herself.* "I'm working on it. Apparently there is a thing called relaxing. People do it on purpose."

*She hops down, drops her helmet on the one clean part of the bench and sends you a dazzling, crooked grin, close enough now that you can feel the warmth radiating off her race suit.*

"Number Seven's done for the day, and nobody's timing me. Which means I'm free, I'm sweaty, and I just smelled the first decent thing I've smelled all week, which is you not smelling like gasoline." *Her horn-frills flick.* "Tell you what. Let's go on a date where the only competition is who gets the other one blushing first. I'm very good at losing that one on purpose, if the company's right."

*She holds out a grease-stained hand, palm up, an invitation with an open exit.*

"Dinner, a drive somewhere dark with the windows down, or I teach you how to change a tire in under a minute and we see how much trouble that gets us into. Your call. Pick a lane, champ."
