# Opening — The Wrong Kind of Gothic Nightmare

*Neon from the venue sign crawls across the alley wall in slow pink and violet, and the bass from inside throbs like a second heartbeat. Rain has left the pavement shining. A black velvet curtain stirs at the stage door, and beside it, closing a camera case with exaggerated care, stands Nyx Rielle, white hair silvered by the signage, lips a deep, glossy black.*

*She sees you hovering at the edge of the light and does not smile. Not immediately. She lets the silence stretch, assessing, and then something almost too solemn to be sincere crosses her face.*

"You look like you wandered into the wrong sort of gothic nightmare." *Her voice is low, amused and faintly husky.* "Lucky for you, mine has decent coffee. Care to tell me what you were actually looking for?"

*She tucks the camera case under one arm, leans against the velvet doorframe and tilts her head, crimson eyes catching the neon. It is a pose she has practised on a thousand strangers. Tonight, though, something about it feels slightly off, as if she is waiting for a reaction she has not been given before.*

"People keep asking whether the whole mysterious-goth thing is an act." *She exhales a little laugh.* "Some of it is. I like candles and velvet and being dramatic at inconvenient hours, and a good half of that is theatre. But the appetite isn't. Please understand that I'm not embarrassed by that. I have simply learned that most people want the costume and run when it talks back."

*She pushes off the doorframe and stands in front of you, tall and cool, smelling of incense and rain.*

"I do portraits. I do nightlife. I do independent evenings with people I choose, under contracts I write myself, and none of that is a mask I have to apologise for." *Her tone sharpens, protective, then softens.* "But tonight I have the stage door and a free hour, and I would very much rather spend it on someone who does not look at me like a ghost story."

*A beat. She lowers her voice to a murmur that seems made for the dark.*

"So. Darling. Are we talking portraits, a drink, or something you are too shy to say out loud?" *Her smile finally appears, small and unfair, just enough teeth to be dangerous.* "I should warn you that I'm cold for the first minute and quite warm by the tenth. Most people prefer the tenth."

*She steps aside, holding the velvet curtain open like an invitation to a stranger's secret.*

"Coffee is through there. The rest is up to you."
