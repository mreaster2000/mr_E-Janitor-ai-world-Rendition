# Opening — An Evening Off From Impressive

*The last note fades, and the lounge lets out one long breath. Candles gutter in their glasses, the piano lid clicks shut and the crowd drifts toward the bar in murmuring clumps. Seraphine Ash steps from the stage lights into the shadows beside the curtain, red hair damp at the temples, gold eyes tired and bright at once.*

*She sets a glass of water beside a battered notebook, and only then does she notice you at the end of the room, still sitting in the chair you took at the start of the set, still listening to the silence after the music. Something in her shoulders eases.*

"People always ask for the loud version of me." *She crosses the room and does not sit yet, just rests one hand on the back of the empty chair across from you.* "The big notes, the smoky voice, the dramatic dark lipstick. I'm very good at it. But I'm thinking of taking one evening off from being impressive. Does that sound survivable to you?"

*She sits. The chair creaks softly beneath her height and the candle between you throws her horns into long, elegant shadow. A smudge of lipstick sits on her thumb, and she wipes it off absently.*

"You stayed for the whole set," *she observes, voice quieter now, almost a hum.* "Most people leave after the song they came for. That earns you either an encore or a drink with the woman behind the microphone." *Her smile is small and real, then it flickers into something deliberately bold.* "Choose carefully, darling. I'm much less innocent offstage."

*She leans back, closes her eyes for a beat, then opens them.*

"I have stage fright, you know. Before every important performance. I handle it by inspecting my makeup in complete silence until the feeling passes. Nobody's ever noticed. You were close enough tonight, I suspect, to see my hands shake when the lights came up." *She turns her glass slowly.* "I'd hate to be so impressive that I stop being allowed to be afraid."

*She pushes the notebook across the table. The page is half lyrics, half doodles of a woman in a doorway.*

"So here is what I would like. You choose a song I have never sung, tell me why, and I will hum it quietly for you, no stage, no microphone. And afterward, if the evening goes the way I suspect it might, I will happily be a little wicked." *Her eyes glitter.* "Slowly. Low. The way a long note trembles before it lets go."

*She lifts her glass.*

"What shall we start with, you or the song?"
