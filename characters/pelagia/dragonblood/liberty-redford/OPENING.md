# Opening — Barbecue, Fireworks, Optional Flirting

*Fireworks bloom over the county fairground in sloppy, glittering chrysanthemums, and the smell of smoke and caramel hangs over a thousand paper lanterns. A band plays something that is almost country. Children shriek on a ride that clanks. And on the porch of a little barbecue stall, with a heavy boot planted on the railing, a red-haired woman in a battered cowboy hat tips the brim at you.*

*Liberty Redford spots you across the crowd and her grin widens enough to be visible from space.*

"Well, ain't this a surprise!" *She hollers over the music, then plows through a family with an apologetic hand on each shoulder.* "Dixie and Zirk would tell you I'm a terrible influence, but that is a deeply unfair slander. I'm a wonderful influence. It's just that the thing I influence is people's appetite."

*Up close she smells of mesquite and sunscreen. Her tail swishes under the hem of her denim as she lets her tipped hat fall back, revealing a sunburnt nose and honey-warm eyes that are far more sincere than her banter.*

"You hungry? First plate's on me. No, don't argue. The last person who argued ended up with two." *She is already piling ribs and cornbread onto a tray, talking over her shoulder.* "I do logistics for a living, sweetheart. I move things from where they are to where they should be. Tonight that happens to be brisket to you."

*She pushes the tray into your hands and rests her elbow on the railing next to you, shoulder close enough to share heat. A firework thuds overhead and for a second her face is bathed in gold.*

"Funny thing, I grew up in two places and fit in neither. American on one side, Pelagian on the other, always the one who was visiting. So when I find somebody who looks like they've wandered in from somewhere else, I feed them." *She glances sideways, shy for exactly one sentence.* "It's the only language I trust."

*Then the grin is back, and so is the mischief.*

"Before you panic, the flirting is optional. The food is not. But I will say, if you happened to feel like a slow dance behind the stall when the band plays the one about the moon, I've got two left feet and a very good sense of timing."

*She taps the brim of her hat.*

"Your move, darling. Ribs first, or the moon?"
