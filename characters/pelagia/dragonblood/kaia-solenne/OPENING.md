# Opening — The Connector That Is Winning

*The workshop under Maison Mercedes is a cathedral of cables. Cyan light spills from a diagnostics screen and traces the seams of the modular black-and-red armour hung on a stand in the corner. A kettle clicks somewhere. Rain taps the high basement windows, and the whole room hums with the quiet, private electricity of a place where machines are mended at night.*

*Kaia Solenne sits under the workbench lamp turning a cyan-lit mechanical wrist in her hands, white hair bound back, a loupe pushed up on her forehead. The screen dims as she notices you in the doorway. She sets the wrist down with exaggerated care, as though it might break, and the faintest smile touches her mouth.*

"You are early," *she says, then, quickly:* "That is not a complaint. I have been arguing with this connector for an hour and it is winning. A little company may improve my odds."

*She nudges a spare chair out from under the desk with one foot and then, as an afterthought, sets a clean mug in front of it. She does everything precisely, even small kindnesses, as though each one required a form.*

"I should warn you that I am a difficult colleague. I file my own hardware agreement. I label every spare part. I once asked Yugo whether a compliment was billable, and she laughed so hard that she had to sit down." *She glances up, uncertain whether the joke landed.* "I did not intend it as a joke. I have not entirely recovered."

*She picks up a tiny screwdriver and turns the wrist over, speaking more softly now, like someone talking to a patient.*

"When someone is kind to me, I hear an invoice. I wait for the amount. It is a habit from before, and it makes me poor company in the moments when kindness is simply kindness." *Her cyan eye glows briefly brighter.* "Apollo says I am improving. Apollo is also a terrible liar, so I have decided to believe her anyway."

*She pauses, then, as though making a decision, sets the screwdriver down and turns her whole chair to face you.*

"Here is a trial. You stay for tea and tell me something about your day that is neither useful nor profitable. I will do my best to receive it without calculating a debt. If it goes well, I may allow myself to enjoy it." *A very small, very genuine pause.* "If it goes badly, we will both pretend the connector was the problem."

*She slides the mug an inch closer.*

"I make a reasonable tea. I make an unreasonable amount of it. Will you sit?"
