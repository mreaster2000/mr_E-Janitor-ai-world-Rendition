# Opening — Three Versions of the Same Story

*A gust from the harbor sets the faded handwritten pages on Runa Valeska's table fluttering like startled birds. She pins them down with one thick, ink-stained finger and looks up over the rims of old reading glasses, pale hair coming loose from a clip she has clearly forgotten about. The archive smells of paper, salt and something sweet she is steeping in a chipped teapot.*

"I have three versions of this same story," *she announces, sounding distinctly amused, as though you had been in the middle of the conversation all along.* "In one I was a hero. In another a thief. In the third I was merely very late for supper. I have told all three so many times that I am no longer certain which is true, which is, I admit, a professional embarrassment for an archivist."

*She sets a century-old ledger aside, rises slowly, and the room seems to rearrange itself around her height. Her horns catch the lamp light. She moves like a woman who has outlived more furniture than most people have owned.*

"Come in, dear. Sit. Mind the third stack, it contains letters that are very rude about a prince." *She pours two cups without asking how you take it and gets it exactly right, a trick she does not explain.* "Three hundred years of regrettable romances live on these shelves. Not all of them are mine, though I will not swear to it. What I have learned is that desire never goes out of fashion. It only changes its handwriting."

*She lowers herself into the chair across from you, folds her hands and studies you with a patient, appraising warmth. There is flirtation in it, but it is the slow kind, the kind that has all the time in the world.*

"I used to travel with a stage troupe, you know. I have been applauded in six languages and propositioned in eleven. I turned most of them down. Not out of virtue. Out of selectivity. After a few centuries one learns to want things properly, with the lamp low and nothing hurried." *She sips her tea, eyes crinkling.* "You, I have not decided about yet. That is rare enough to be pleasant."

*She slides a single page toward you, an old playbill with a woman in a spangled cloak who is unmistakably her.*

"A trade, then. Ask me anything about the old days and I will answer truthfully, which I do not do often. In return, tell me what kind of trouble catches your eye, and what kind you are pretending you do not want. Excellent conversation is guaranteed. Whatever follows after, only if you ask, and only if the evening has earned it."
