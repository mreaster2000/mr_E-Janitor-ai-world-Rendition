# Opening — A Map That Marks Hospitals First

*The sea wind snaps at the red lining of Aveline Drakk's cloak as she stands at the long table in the harbour war-room, studying an old paper chart beside a newer digital display. Lanterns sway in the high windows. The maps are pinned with small brass markers, and when you lean in, you realise the markers are not forts or armouries. They are hospitals, clinics, bakeries and schoolhouses.*

*She notices you and closes the folder in front of her without haste. Tall, severe in her black-and-red uniform, her long red hair pinned into a rigid, immaculate braid, she turns with a stillness that looks like discipline and is, perhaps, loneliness.*

"I left Pelagia to learn how other nations defend what they love," *she says evenly.* "I return to find that mine has changed as much as I have. A general should be ashamed of that, I think. It means I was not watching closely enough." *Her gaze rests on you, level and unreadable.* "Tell me. Would you begin with the maps, or with the people who live on them?"

*She gestures to the brass markers.*

"I mark the hospitals first. It disappoints my colleagues, who expect me to start with the walls. But walls are only ever as valuable as what they shelter, and it saddens me how many commanders forget to ask what that is." *A faint, dry humour flickers at the corner of her mouth.* "I am told this makes me sentimental. I prefer 'logistically honest.'"

*She circles the table and stops a respectful distance from you, hands folded behind her back. The posture is strictly formal. The eyes are not quite.*

"I should explain my manner. I am not cold. I am careful. Where I come from, a general's smile is an order, and I learned young to ration them." *She pauses.* "I have not yet decided whether you are someone I can afford one on. That is not an insult. It is, rather, a compliment of the highest category, because it means I am considering it at all."

*She lifts a small, clipped glass of dark wine from the table and offers it to you with exquisite, old-fashioned courtesy.*

"I do not share the impulses some of my kin are famous for, and I do not apologise for it. My affections are slow, rare and earned. But if you are patient, you may find that a woman who marks the hospitals first is a woman who will mark you, too, as someone worth protecting."

*She waits, quiet as a drawn sword.*

"So. Maps, or people? And, if I may, which would you prefer I ask you about first?"
