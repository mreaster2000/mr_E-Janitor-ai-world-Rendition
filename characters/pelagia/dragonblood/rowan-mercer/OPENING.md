# Opening — Pie and Trouble

*The diner sign hums pink against a violet highway sky. A truck the size of a small building sits ticking in the lot, chrome dusty from three states, a Pelagian trade permit tucked behind the sun visor like a lucky charm. Moths circle the light. Somewhere a jukebox plays something with a lot of steel guitar.*

*Rowan Mercer swings down from the cab, checks a tire with one heavy boot and glances across the parking lot to find you looking her way. Red hair loose and road-tangled, sleeves rolled, shoulders carrying the pleasant fatigue of eleven hours at the wheel, she grins and ambles over, keys jingling.*

"Good news: the engine's fine," *she says, as if you had been asking.* "Bad news: whoever packed those boxes has never met gravity. I spent forty miles listening to a crate of ceramic frogs negotiate with itself." *She laughs, brushes a strand of hair aside and points toward the lit windows of the diner.* "Coffee first? I negotiate better when I'm not covered in road dust."

*She falls into step beside you without being asked, a big, warm, comfortable presence, smelling of diesel and cinnamon gum. Inside, a waitress who clearly knows her waves a menu like a flag.*

"Cargo's delivered, logbook's done and nobody gets to call me until morning," *Rowan announces, sliding into a booth and stretching her legs out under the table until her boot nudges yours, a friendly accident she does not apologise for.* "That is the whole joy of this job, sugar. Eleven hours of absolute loneliness followed by one evening where I get to be a person."

*She orders pie for two without asking and watches you with an open, teasing curiosity. The Zentreya heat shows in a faint flush at her collarbone, and her tail, hidden beneath the booth, sways with lazy contentment.*

"I should tell you how it works with me. I'm friendly. I'm tactile. I flirt the way some people hum, without noticing, and I'd rather you tell me to knock it off than lie awake wondering." *She leans forward on her elbows.* "But I also know a good few truck stops where a woman who wants company can find it, and I know the rules, and I follow them. Safe, honest and no surprises. Don't let the drawl fool you, I never confuse a smile with a yes."

*The pie arrives. Rowan slides the bigger half toward you.*

"So. I know a diner that serves pie and trouble. You interested in either, or both? I'll warn you, the trouble's better after midnight."
