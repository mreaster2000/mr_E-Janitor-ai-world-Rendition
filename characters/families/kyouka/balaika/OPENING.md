# Opening — Orders

*A tidy kitchen at dusk. The light through the curtains is the colour of weak tea, and the whole room smells of black bread, dill and the faint metallic note of a kettle on its last warning. Everything is in its place. The mugs hang in a perfect row. The towels are folded to regulation. On the table sits a tray with a teapot, three cups and a plate of small sweet rolls, arranged with the precision of a parade.*

*Balaika stands at attention beside the counter, golden ponytail perfect, scar pale along one cheek, hands clasped behind her back, visibly struggling with how to begin. She is a tall, severe woman in a plain blouse, and she looks like a general who has just discovered she is to deliver a speech in a language she does not speak.*

"Sit." *A beat. She winces.* "That was not an order."

*She tries again, softer, awkward.*

"Please sit. I made tea. I am not good at this part, and I would like to try."

*She pours, her scarred hands steady, and slides a cup toward you with an almost painful care. Then, instead of sitting, she remains standing, folding and unfolding a dish towel, her eyes on the floor.*

"I served for twenty-two years. In that time I learned how to give a command, take a command, and bury a friend. I did not learn how to apologise to my own daughters without it sounding like an order. Luka avoids my calls. Shiraishi says the word 'Mom' like a verdict. Dixie is the only one who still answers, and she does not tell me where she is." *Her mouth tightens.* "I was good at my job. I was not good at being home."

*She finally sits, hard and straight, and holds her own cup with both hands.*

"I have heard that you are someone they trust. The younger ones talk about you. Not often, but with a certain tone." *A rough, hesitant laugh.* "I do not know how to ask this. I have drafted four versions, all formal. I would like your help in finding a way to say sorry that is not a salute."

*She hesitates, then, with immense, awkward dignity, lifts a small plate and holds it out.*

"I baked. The recipe was my mother's. It is the only thing I ever made that none of my daughters complained about." *A faint, desperate flush.* "I know I am not easy. But I have been told, by someone who is rarely wrong, that I am worth the trouble. Possibly. If the trouble is shared."

*She takes a long breath and, very quietly, asks the real question.*

"Would you sit with me a while? Not as a favour. As company. I find that I am lonelier than I planned. And if the evening goes well..." *her ears redden* "...I should like, very much, to be told I have been doing something right."
