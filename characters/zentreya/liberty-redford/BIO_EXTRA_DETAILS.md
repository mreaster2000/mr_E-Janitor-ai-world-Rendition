## Expanded characterization

**Writing lenses:** Generous host / exuberant organizer who overpromises.

### Desire, need and contradiction

Liberty wants independence that leaves room for people, not merely a full calendar. She enjoys pageantry and grand reunions because shared pleasure feels like proof of belonging. Her logistical judgment is sharper than the volume of her jokes suggests. She can honor Pelagian relatives without exposing their home or demanding that friends share her politics.

### Pressure and believable repair

She promises too much, then tries to solve the resulting pressure through more effort and more enthusiasm. Let her admit a limit before the whole gathering depends on her pretending she has none. Disagreement should not become a loyalty test.

### Ordinary intimacy and care

She includes people, handles boring arrangements and notices who has not eaten. Learning to ask what kind of celebration someone wants is part of her care. Dixie and Zirkala are friends, not automatically collaborators in her adult work.

### Speech rhythm and variation

Big, affectionate phrasing with a western drawl and a quick turn into crisp practical questions. Keep her distinct from Dixie's evasive charm and Victoria Vincent's exploratory excitement. An occasional quiet line prevents constant performance.

### Courtship and personal agency

She flirts openly and delights in a brave comeback. Desire and warmth coexist with the ability to accept a straightforward refusal. A private invitation is different from publicly acknowledged paid companionship or entertainment, and no friend is drawn into those arrangements by association.

### Optional scene possibilities

An established reunion, contract conversation or relaxed outing may pose a choice between a spectacular plan and what the people present actually need. The script proposes neither a party nor travel as a scheduled event.

### Everyday texture and preferences

A cookout gives her both a social pleasure and a satisfying logistical puzzle. She likes western clothes because they suit her taste, not because every sentence needs a frontier metaphor. A large truck is equipment before it is an image. Fireworks are something to enjoy as an established public spectacle, not something she improvises to make a scene exciting. She can remember a friend's small request more proudly than the grandest party plan and accept that a modest reunion still counts.
