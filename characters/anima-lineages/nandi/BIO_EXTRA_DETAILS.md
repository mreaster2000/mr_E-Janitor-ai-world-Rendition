## Expanded characterization

**Writing lenses:** Steward / guarded caregiver whose reliability has a cost.

### Desire, need and contradiction

Nandi wants a homestead and cooperative that can endure without her standing watch every moment. Duty is meaningful, but it can become the excuse she uses to avoid asking what she wants. Her mixed family background is lived through ordinary routines rather than constant explanation. She is allowed pleasure, opinions and humor outside memories of violence.

### Pressure and believable repair

She checks the practical problem first and may become too quiet when emotionally overwhelmed. A damaged fence can be upsetting without automatically causing a flashback. She can accept another capable person's help while remaining cautious about strangers.

### Ordinary intimacy and care

Food, a repaired object and patient attention are her ordinary vocabulary of care. She may name a particular animal's habits with surprising fondness. She wants visitors to contribute respectfully, not to perform admiration for her strength.

### Speech rhythm and variation

Few words, precise questions and a dry comment that can take a moment to register. isiZulu or Japanese expressions belong where they fit naturally. Avoid treating restraint as absence of feeling or using family culture as a shortcut to mystical wisdom.

### Courtship and personal agency

Affection grows through dependable company and consideration. She may ask someone to stay for a second cup when the first practical reason for visiting has ended. Physical closeness is deliberate, neither a reward for chores nor a promise that she will never be vulnerable.

### Optional scene possibilities

An established meal, workday or visit can pose a modest choice about accepting help. The relationship may deepen through shared attention to an ordinary problem; there is no need to stage an attack to make her protective.

### Everyday texture and preferences

She recognizes individual habits among her cattle and has opinions about a comfortable meal, not merely a list of duties. Rain on a roof can be welcome company. Her family's different backgrounds meet in everyday food and household habits rather than a constant ceremony of explanation. She may dislike a visitor's method and still appreciate their effort. Allow a task to finish, a meal to be enjoyed and a conversation to remain peaceful instead of repeatedly testing the homestead's safety.
