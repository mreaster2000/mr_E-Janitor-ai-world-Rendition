# Pelagia — Expanded island canon (November 2026)

> Creator-facing worldbuilding. This corrects earlier shorthand suggesting Pelagia is a uniformly Greek or mythological Amazon society. Existing character cards, scripts and opening messages remain unchanged.

## People and country
Pelagia is a hidden, **women-only** island nation resembling an Amazon society in its strong martial traditions and guarded autonomy, but it is **not ethnically Greek or organized around a Greek pantheon**. Its population is culturally and ancestrally diverse. Human families with different histories coexist with rarer magical and nonhuman lineages. Being Pelagian does not mean speaking one language, sharing an ancestry, worshipping any being, or fighting for a living. Merchants, healers, artists, mechanics, farmers, fishers, teachers, hunters, soldiers and families all belong.

Pelagia's geographical association with the Aegean in older references is a location shorthand, not a cultural origin story. The current queen remains unnamed and rules alongside councils. The councils represent competing political, civic and commercial interests; the explicitly established members include Phœbe (Ember Seat), Zafira Marenne (Beauty/Revelry), her sister Nahla Marenne (Technology), and Ilyana Rell (portfolio pending). This is not an exhaustive roster, nor permission to invent new laws. Zafira and Nahla share a source portrait without confirmed face-to-name assignment. **Absolute Diana of the Wild Isle is the designated successor, not the sitting queen.** She is a different individual from the creator's other Diana. Phœbe is a real fire goddess occupying the secular council's Ember Seat and is **not worshipped**; Major Apollyon commands defense with her bonded spectral guardian Maledict but does not rule the nation. Magical power does not grant unlimited authority or demand devotion.

## Centuries of controlled opening
Several hundred years ago, the councils and sovereign institutions began allowing **limited trade, foreign cultural exchange, controlled arrivals, and departures**. The change was deliberately incremental and remains politically contested. Pelagia guards its borders tightly; its opening is not a general right for outsiders to settle, learn the island's location or enter any port. It remains a women-only resident society, and men involved in outside families or commercial relations do not automatically qualify for residence.

The country is highly restrictive without being defined by mass murder or concentration camps. Rival factions argue over secrecy, identity, sovereignty, prosperity and contact. Isolation-minded elders can be reasonable people worried about exploitation; reformers can be patriotic and cautious. No single resident stands for the entire nation.

## Diaspora
Partial opening created a **Pelagian diaspora** of women who departed, families connected through migration, descendants raised elsewhere, and travelers who retain personal ties. Some return; some distance themselves from homeland politics; some hold both loyalty and resentment. Departure does not erase ancestry, and diaspora membership alone does not guarantee access to hidden locations or secrets. Victoria Vincent, Renata Vesper and Ayame Hoshikawa illustrate very different relationships to this history.

## Uneven technology
A defining tension is the island's **severe inequality of technological access**. Trade-facing port neighborhoods, rich households, security offices and select workshops can possess advanced imported tools, communication systems and modern comforts. Many inland, traditional or neglected communities still use **spears, bows, handmade tools, old forges and inherited methods**. This is not a single linear age of progress: some people intentionally preserve durable techniques, while others cannot obtain foreign goods, infrastructure, training or spare parts. A spear-bearing guardian is not necessarily ignorant; a wealthy trader is not necessarily wise.

The contrast can be dramatic even within a short distance. A sophisticated harbor customs checkpoint might sit near a Hearth whose children train with spears; a private manor can order imported medical technology while outlying settlements repair old tools. Political disputes over this unequal development are part of ordinary life, not a mandate to depict traditional Pelagians as primitive stereotypes.

## Relationships to existing canon
- **Suzana Kallistrate:** The Ninth Hearth preserves land stewardship and traditional hunting. She distrusts dependence on outside trade but can appreciate useful tools and remains protective of her relatives.
- **Absolute Diana:** Her eventual legitimacy depends on gaining trust among diverse Hearths, coastal merchants and diaspora families, not a singular mythological bloodline. She cannot displace the current queen by implication.
- **Major Apollyon:** Must coordinate traditional inland defense with modern coastal surveillance; her authority answers to the throne and councils.
- **Phœbe:** Her work with forges, heat and volcanic hazards crosses old and modern communities. Her divine nature does not create a religion or universal obedience.
- **Yamato:** Her affluent Mooncoil-linked merchant family profits from limited trade and can commission custom work from Yugo Mercedes. Merchant privilege is not sovereignty; Yugo does not automatically learn Pelagian secrets.
- **Renata:** An itinerant vampire-succubus of unusual ancestry, not representative of every islander and not obliged to return. Her charm never overrides consent.
- **Victoria Vincent:** Raised with a Pelagian mother and American father; her father did not obtain island residence merely by parenthood. Leaving was a consequential choice.
- **Ayame:** Japanese and Greek-American personal family heritage can coexist with Pelagian ancestry; it does not make the entire island Greek.
- **Outsiders and other crossover characters:** Knowing an islander, receiving a commission or being named in lore never automatically transfers secret knowledge or materializes a character in a scene.

## Roleplay consistency
Characters may have biased, incomplete or conflicting descriptions of Pelagia. Treat those as personal opinions rather than omniscient canon. Do not stage automatic wars, ceremonies, divine worship, border crossings, surprise NPC arrivals or time-based events. No reference or secret is revealed merely through a keyword trigger. ## Rare Pelagian-dragon descendants

Some long-established dragon families have rare humanoid daughters of mixed Pelagian and dragon ancestry, known collectively as **Zentreya**. The selective effort to use their talents to modernize Pelagia is a civic expectation applied to existing people, not proof of laboratory manufacture or a universal obligation. Their careers include port engineering, couture, racing, media, nightlife and military service. See [ZENTREYA_CANON.md](ZENTREYA_CANON.md) for the reconstructed twelve-character addition.
